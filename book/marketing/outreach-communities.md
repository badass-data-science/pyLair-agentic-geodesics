# Communities to Tell About the Book

These are the communities I'd suggest, grouped by why they'd care. I drew them from memory rather than researching them, so before sending anything, check that each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion. Many forums allow announcements only in a designated thread.

## Geodesic dome builders and enthusiasts

This is the most direct fit, since the book designs a real dome end to end.

- **Domerama** (domerama.com): a long-running geodesic dome calculator site with a builders' community. Its readers already think in strut lengths and hub angles.
- **Desert Domes** (desertdomes.com): a well-known hobbyist dome-building resource. Its audience builds the kinds of domes Chapters 12 to 15 cut templates for.
- **Burning Man and festival dome-building circles:** people who really do cut struts and panels in bulk, the readers Chapters 20 and 22 (nesting and assembly) serve.

## Buckminster Fuller and synergetics communities

- **The Buckminster Fuller Institute.** The book builds on Fuller's work.
- **The Synergetics Collaborative:** a group devoted to Fuller's geometry, interested in subdivision classes and chirality.

## Computational design in architecture

- **The Grasshopper and Rhino community** (McNeel's forum, food4Rhino): parametric designers who would value a scriptable, agent-driven alternative.
- **ACADIA** (the Association for Computer Aided Design in Architecture). Its audience is academic, so lead with the rigour (the independent checks and the supplementary scripts) rather than the jokes.
- **The International Association for Shell and Spatial Structures (IASS):** the professional home for spatial structures. Be explicit, as Chapter 11 is, that pyLair checks geometry, not structural engineering.

## Mathematics and geometry

- **The Bridges Organization** (mathematics and art): a natural audience for Goldberg–Coxeter subdivision, chirality and the no-fit polygon, presented playfully.
- **The author of `antitile`:** Chapter 6 credits that library as an independent cross-check, and an author is usually glad to hear their code mattered.

## Fabrication and making

- **Fab Labs and makerspaces** (the Fab Foundation network): laser and CNC users, the people for pyFit's nesting chapters.
- **Hackaday:** it takes submitted tips, and an agent-designed volcano lair is the kind of project it covers.
- **Users of SVGnest and Deepnest:** the open-source nesting tools the book compares pyFit to.

## Python, MCP and AI agents

These readers come for the "agentic" half of the title.

- **MCP server directories,** for example the community "awesome MCP servers" lists on GitHub. pyLair and pyFit would belong there in their own right, and could link to the book.
- **Python newsletters** such as PyCoder's Weekly and Python Weekly, which take reader submissions.

## Two practical suggestions

- **Fit the pitch to the group.** Dome builders want the templates and the bill-of-materials workflow. Academics want the checking method (Chapter 24). Makers want the nesting results. Agent and MCP readers want the iterate-then-commit tool design (Chapters 18 and 21).
- **Plan for builders taking the book literally.** Builder communities may treat it as engineering guidance. The book already says pyLair confirms geometry, not that a structure will survive loads, so bring that caveat into any builder-facing announcement.
