# A Word on AI Use

pyLair was written by hand from scratch, then extended feature-by-feature in collaboration with an AI coding assistant — Class II and III subdivision, elongation, STL/OBJ/PNG output, and face-aware truncation among them. pyFit, unlike its sibling, was designed and built collaboratively with an AI coding assistant from a standing start: our heroine scoped the requirements and reviewed the results, while the actual module layout, the NFP algorithm, the Minkowski-sum bug and its fix, and the test suite were all written the other way around.

This book's own prose follows the same pattern its two subjects do: drafted collaboratively, then checked line-by-line against the actual codebases, their real test suites, and their real git history — not invented, and not taken on faith just because an earlier draft said so. Where that checking turned up a real surprise along the way, the relevant chapter says so plainly rather than quietly fixing the outline and moving on.

And no, the villain never generated the cover art. She hired an actual henchman for that.
